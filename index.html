03 — THE SIGNAL

Case notes from the frontier

Real projects and papers, credited and linked. What it is, why it matters, and one honest limit.

PAPER · MIT Center for Bits and Atoms · Aug 2026

Robots that build reconfigurable structures from self-aligning lattice modules

What: Kyaw, Smith, Richard and Gershenfeld design a cuboctahedral–octahedral module whose geometry gives robots surfaces to grasp and self-align, with screw-releasable snap-fit connectors so structures can be taken apart and reassembled.

Why it matters: the material system and the robot are designed together, which makes simple robots precise. Limit: structures are stiff lattices that rely on connectors rather than structural form.

Read the paper on arXiv →

RESEARCH FUNDING · ICD, University of Stuttgart · Oct 2026

Teams of robots for real construction sites: SCALAR and COBRAS

What: two EIC Pathfinder projects coordinated by ICD, each funded with about €4 million and starting November 2026, on autonomous multi-robot systems for unstructured sites.

Why it matters: moves construction robotics from the lab cell to the messy building site. Limit: these are new projects; results are still to come.

Read the announcement →

PROJECT · Polyhedral Structures Lab, UPenn

A 3D-printed, compression-dominant concrete floor

What: a funicular floor printed with a 5-axis robot arm from 24 segments plus four capitals, using dual-banded post-tensioning so forces travel efficiently in both directions.

Why it matters: geometry does the structural work, cutting concrete and steel. Limit: columns still use printed shells as lost formwork around conventional reinforced concrete.

06 — ABOUT

Made by an architect learning to build with robots

UN/BUILT is created by Chi Lee, an architectural designer based in Barcelona. Chi trained in Robotics and Advanced Construction at IAAC and completed the MADE master's at UPC, working on robotic fabrication, 3D-printed prototypes and robotic 3D scanning in team and individual projects.

The name is a promise: start from what is not built yet, and explain the engineering honestly. Episodes use computed simulations and say so; claims link to primary sources; other people's work is credited, never re-uploaded.
